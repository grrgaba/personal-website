October 17–21, 2024 · Oʻahu

Hawaii

We left Edmonton on a Thursday and did not really stop until a rock at Lāʻie Point. The first two days are the story. The last two are the pictures, until I write the rest.

Thursday

Departure, and a layover we spent outside

The flight out of Edmonton was at 10. YEG to YVR, and then a wait I did not want to spend under airport lights. The book was already open at the gate.

In Vancouver we rented a car and drove south to Steveston. Gulf of Georgia Cannery first, the national historic site on the water, and then lunch while we still had the car. Two bowls of hot pot, the cannery buildings sitting across the street.

Garry Point Park was the sweet part of the layover. A picnic table facing the water, mountains on the far shore, and the two of us walking the driftwood with Vancouver only a faint line behind us. Then the clock ran out. At 5 we were back at YVR to meet Peter. I slept on the flight after that. The next time I was really awake, the Pacific was under us and Oʻahu was close.

Friday

The night, the almost, and Lāʻie Point

We landed around 11 and did not go to bed. The island was right there, so we went out into it. Around 1 we were on the rocks at Diamond Head Beach Park. After that we looked at crabs at Maunalua Bay Beach Park, then stood at Koko Kai Beach Mini Park trying to find Spitting Cave in the dark. We never did. China Walls was the same: we looked, and the dark looked back.

Lānai Lookout at least gave us the lava shelf, and a flashlight. Later we were crabbing again, on the beach near Sandy Beach, the beam doing all the work.

After midnight

Byodo-In, once it opened

Near Heʻeia we tried to rinse off at a gym. It did not work out that way. We rested there for about two hours, until Byodo-In opened, and the morning turned into a pavilion, a still pond, and a red bridge with the mountains behind it.

Kualoa, instead of Secret Island

I had a plan for Secret Island. It was full of people, and there was a private party, so we let it go and went to Kualoa Regional Park. Ironwoods along the bay, the little island offshore, the Koʻolau cliffs standing straight up behind the grass. We washed up there too, as much as a park and a bottle of water allow.

Behind the park there is a field. That was supposed to be the place. I had the ring. There were not even that many people, and I still could not do it. Too seen. Too nervous. We took the pictures and we moved on.

Lāʻie Point

The backup in my head was the Polynesian Cultural Center, and I already knew that was wrong for this. She would have hated a crowd for it. On the drive east I was so wound up I could not get the words out at any of the pullouts. There was one stop left before the center.

Lāʻie Point State Wayside. The sun was right. The water was an impossible color. The waves stood up and broke like they meant it. We took a lot of pictures. I kept telling Mike to photograph Sarah, that we had time before the center. I was buying time. I was looking at her in that white and red dress and thinking that if I walked away from this rock I would regret it for the rest of my life.

I walked back to the car. Peter was in it with the air conditioning on, because it was hot, just sitting there. I got the ring. I gave Mike my phone and said, bro, pa-record.

On the walk back I tried to guess which would come first, the laugh or the cry. I know her. I knew she would laugh from the shock, hard, and then she would cry.

I got her attention. I knelt on rock that was sharper than I expected. I asked. She laughed exactly the way I thought she would. She said yes, quietly, and she cried, and she hugged me without really looking at the ring. I still cry when I think about it. I do not think that is going to stop.

Polynesian Cultural Center

We went to the Polynesian Cultural Center after that, which turned out to be the right place to land. We went through the villages, took the canoe, sat for the temporary tattoos, and stayed for dinner and the evening show. The knowledge, the food, the noise, all of it. I could finally watch her smile without rehearsing a sentence in my head. She was happy. She was not going to forget it either. I had asked. Best day of my life.

We slept at a sketchy campsite and called that the start of the next chapter. I did not take a picture of the campsite.
